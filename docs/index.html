Ask questions about our website traffic

This connects Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor to Google Search Console, Google Analytics and Mailchimp for stgconsulting.com. Once it is set up you can ask, in ordinary English, what people search for to find us, which pages are growing and which are slipping, and how our email campaigns landed.

You sign in with the same work Google account you use for email. There is nothing to install, and you cannot break the website with it.

Setting it up

About a minute, and only once for each app you use. All you need is the link from the top of this page.

In Claude

  1. Open Settings, then Connectors.

    In the desktop app this sits under the Claude menu. On the website it is behind your name in the bottom-left corner.

  2. Choose Add custom connector.

  3. Paste the link from the top of this page, and save.

    Use the Copy button so you do not lose a character. Leave every other box empty.

  4. Choose Connect, and sign in with your work Google account.

    The same account you use for email. Claude will ask once; after that it remembers.

  5. Ask your first question.

    An entry called seo appears. The first time you ask something, Claude will check that it is allowed to use it — choose Allow for this chat.

In other apps

ChatGPT, Cursor, Windsurf and Cline all accept the same link. Look for “custom connector”, “remote MCP server” or “add integration” in their settings. Each one will send you to the same Google sign-in the first time you use it.

Ask in an ordinary chat. If you are in a Cowork or Code session and it reports the connector as offline, that is a quirk of those session types rather than a fault. Start a normal chat and it will be there.

What it is connected to

Three places, read directly. Nothing is copied into a spreadsheet first, so an answer is whatever those three say at the moment you ask.

Search Console
What people typed into Google, how often we appeared, how often they clicked, and where we ranked. Also whether a page is in Google at all.
Analytics
What visitors did once they arrived — the page they landed on, how engaged they were, and what they went on to do.
Mailchimp
Every campaign we have sent, who opened and clicked, who unsubscribed, and which links in a message got the clicks.

What you can ask

  • This campaign sent people to a page — how does that compare with its search traffic?
  • Which pages get email traffic but almost nothing from Google?
  • We emailed about a service last month. Did searches for it go up afterwards?

Asking it things

Just ask. You never have to name the website or paste any kind of identifier — it already knows which site it is looking at.

  • Which pages lost the most visitors in the last 28 days compared with the 28 before?
  • What are our top 20 search terms this month, and where do we rank for each?
  • Which pages show up high in Google but hardly get clicked?
  • How is our fractional CTO page doing?
  • Has Google indexed this page yet?
  • How did our last email campaign do, and which links did people click?
  • Which of our audiences has the best open rate?
  • Write a short summary of last month's search performance for the team.

Follow-up questions work normally. Ask it to look closer at one page, change the dates, or put the answer in a table.

Reading the numbers

Three things that will otherwise catch you out. The connector mentions all of them in its answers, but they are easy to skim past.

A list of search terms never adds up to the total

This matters more than it sounds. Never work out a percentage from a list of search terms — “40% of our traffic comes from X”, calculated that way, will be wrong. Totals by page are unaffected; it is only search terms that are hidden.

Google's figures run two to three days behind

Ask about yesterday and you will often see nothing, or a number that later goes up. That is normal. Every answer states the exact dates it used, so check that line whenever a figure looks surprising. Search Console keeps roughly 16 months of history, so comparisons with two years ago are not possible.

Analytics and Search Console count different things

Search Console counts clicks that came from Google. Analytics counts visits from everywhere, in our own timezone. The two will never match exactly, and that is expected rather than a fault.

What the four main figures mean

A page with many impressions and very few clicks is usually a title problem rather than a ranking problem: Google is already showing it to people, and they are choosing something else.

Two things worth knowing

Email click counts are inflated. Company mail scanners open and click every link in a message before anyone sees it, so a campaign can record three times as many clicks as it had readers. The connector says so in its answers and points at the number that counts actual people — trust that one, and treat per-link totals as a ranking rather than a tally.

Everyone shares one allowance from Google. The only limit you could realistically reach is on “is this page indexed?” questions — about two thousand a day across the whole team. Asking about hundreds of pages in one sitting could use it up for everyone. Everything else has far more room than we will ever need.

Read-only by design. The connector can look at Search Console and Analytics and nothing else — it has no ability to change the website, and the part of it that could was switched off before anyone was given the link.
